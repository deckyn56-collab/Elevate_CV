10 Resume Mistakes That Are Costing You Interviews

August 30, 2026

I remember the frustration of sending out dozens of resumes and never hearing back. After months of trial and error, I realized the problem wasn't my experience—it was the mistakes on my resume. Even a single error could cost me an interview.

In this guide, I'll reveal the 10 most common resume mistakes that are silently killing your job search. Whether you're a recent graduate or a seasoned professional, fixing these mistakes could be the difference between being ignored and landing your dream job.

1. Using a Generic Resume for Every Job

This is the #1 mistake job seekers make. Sending the same resume to every employer shows a lack of effort. Recruiters can tell when a resume isn't tailored to their specific job posting.

Fix: Tailor your resume to each job description, matching keywords and emphasizing relevant experience.

2. Including Irrelevant Information

Do recruiters really need to know your high school GPA or that you enjoy watching TV? Unrelated information clutters your resume and distracts from your qualifications.

Fix: Cut anything that doesn't directly support your ability to do the job.

3. Using a Weak Summary Statement

A generic statement like "I am a hardworking individual looking for a job" says nothing. Your summary should be a powerful elevator pitch that highlights your unique value.

Fix: Write a compelling professional summary that includes your years of experience, key skills, and top achievements.

4. Listing Duties Instead of Achievements

Simply listing your job responsibilities doesn't show what you accomplished. Employers want to know the impact you made, not just what you were supposed to do.

Fix: Use action verbs and quantify your achievements. For example, "Increased sales by 20% through targeted marketing campaigns."

5. Using "I" Statements

Using phrases like "I was responsible for" or "I managed" is redundant and takes up space. Employers know you're talking about yourself.

Fix: Drop "I" and start with action verbs like "Managed", "Developed", or "Led".

6. Including Typos and Grammar Errors

A single typo can make you look careless. In a competitive job market, small mistakes can cost you the interview.

Fix: Proofread your resume multiple times. Ask a friend or use a tool like Grammarly to catch errors.

7. Using Unprofessional Email Addresses

Using an email like "partygirl_99@email.com" immediately undermines your professionalism.

Fix: Create a professional email address using your name, such as "john.doe@email.com".

8. Including Personal Information That Shouldn't Be There

In the US, it's illegal for employers to ask about age, marital status, or religion. Including this information on your resume can actually hurt your chances.

Fix: Remove age, marital status, and any personal information that could lead to discrimination.

9. Not Including Keywords from the Job Description

Many companies use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to filter resumes. If your resume doesn't contain keywords from the job description, it will be automatically rejected.

Fix: Review the job description and incorporate relevant keywords into your resume.

10. Making it Too Long

In the US, a one-page resume is preferred for early-career professionals, while experienced professionals can use two pages. Anything longer is overwhelming.

Fix: Keep your resume concise. Focus on the most relevant experience and achievements.

Summary

Your resume is your first impression. By avoiding these 10 common mistakes, you'll significantly increase your chances of landing interviews and advancing your career.

FAQ

1. How do I know if my resume has typos?

Proofread multiple times or use a tool like Grammarly to catch errors.

2. Should I include my GPA on my resume?

Only if it's above 3.5 and you're a recent graduate with limited experience.

3. How many pages should my resume be?

One page for early-career professionals, two pages for experienced professionals.

4. Do I need a professional summary?

Yes! A strong professional summary makes you stand out and gives recruiters a quick overview.

5. How do I tailor my resume to a job?

Read the job description and incorporate relevant keywords and experience into your resume.
