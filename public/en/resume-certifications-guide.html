How to Include Certifications on Your Resume (2026 Guide)

September 21, 2026

Certifications are like proof of your skills. They show employers that you've invested time to learn and validate your knowledge. In 2026, having the right certifications can be the difference between getting an interview or getting ignored.

But how exactly do you include certifications on your resume? Where do you put them? What format should you use? Let me show you everything you need to know.

1. Where to Put Certifications on Your Resume

There are 3 common places to list certifications:

  1. Dedicated "Certifications" section – Best if you have 3+ relevant certifications.
  2. Under "Education" section – Good for fresh graduates or if you have only 1-2 certifications.
  3. In your "Skills" section – Best if the certification is directly related to a specific skill.

2. The Correct Format for Listing Certifications

Use this format for each certification:

Certification Name – Issuing Organization (Year)

Example:

  • Google Data Analytics Professional Certificate – Google (2025)
  • Project Management Professional (PMP) – PMI (2026)
  • Certified Public Accountant (CPA) – AICPA (2024)

3. What Certifications Are Valuable in 2026?

  • ✅ Google Data Analytics – High demand for data skills
  • ✅ PMP (Project Management) – For leadership roles
  • ✅ AWS Certified Solutions Architect – For tech roles
  • ✅ HubSpot Content Marketing – For marketing roles
  • ✅ CompTIA Security+ – For cybersecurity roles
  • ✅ Microsoft Azure Fundamentals – For cloud computing

4. Should You Include Certifications That Are Not Relevant?

No. Only include certifications that are relevant to the job you're applying for. Irrelevant ones just take up space and dilute your resume.

5. How to Highlight Certifications When You Have No Experience

If you're a fresh graduate, certifications can be your greatest asset. Place them right after your education and write a brief description of what you learned.

Example:

Certifications

  • Google Digital Marketing Certificate – Google (2025): Completed 7-course program covering SEO, Google Ads, and analytics.

6. How to List Pending or In-Progress Certifications

If you're currently pursuing a certification, you can add it with the phrase "(In Progress)" or "(Expected 2026)".

Example: "AWS Solutions Architect – Amazon Web Services (In Progress, Expected December 2026)"

7. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • ❌ Listing expired certifications without noting the date
  • ❌ Adding certifications without the issuing organization
  • ❌ Including irrelevant certifications
  • ❌ Using long descriptions for every certification
  • ❌ Lying about certifications you don't have

8. Example Resume Section (Copy This)

Certifications

  • Google Data Analytics Professional Certificate – Google (2025)
  • Project Management Professional (PMP) – PMI (2026)
  • HubSpot Content Marketing Certification – HubSpot (2025)

9. Should You Put Certifications in Your Resume Title?

If the certification is a major requirement for the job (e.g., CPA, PMP), you can add it after your name in the header.

Example: "John Doe, PMP | Data Analyst"

10. Final Checklist Before Submitting

  1. ✅ Is each certification relevant to the job?
  2. ✅ Did I include the issuing organization?
  3. ✅ Did I include the year?
  4. ✅ Is the formatting consistent?
  5. ✅ Did I avoid expired or irrelevant ones?

Final Thoughts

Certifications are a simple and effective way to boost your credibility without needing years of experience. List them properly, and you'll stand out from the competition.

FAQ

1. Should I include certifications on my resume?

Yes, if they're relevant to the job. They validate your skills and show initiative.

2. How do I format certifications on my resume?

Use this format: Certification Name – Issuing Organization (Year).

3. Can I include online courses as certifications?

Yes, if they're from reputable platforms like Google, Coursera, or HubSpot and you completed them.

4. How many certifications should I list?

3-5 relevant certifications is ideal. Quality over quantity.

5. What if my certification is expired?

Either exclude it or clearly indicate the expiration date. Most employers prefer valid certifications.
