How to Write a Cover Letter for US Jobs (2026 Guide)

October 1, 2026

The US job market has its own unique culture, expectations, and norms. If you're a non-US candidate applying for US jobs—or even if you're American but haven't applied in a while—understanding these differences is critical.

In this guide, I'll show you how to write a cover letter for US jobs in 2026 that meets American expectations and impresses recruiters.

Understanding US Job Market Culture

Before you write, it's important to understand a few things about the US job market:

  • Direct and professional tone: Avoid being overly formal or indirect. Be confident and concise.
  • Results-oriented: US employers want to know what you've achieved, not just what you've done.
  • Personal branding: Highlighting your unique value is encouraged, not seen as bragging.
  • Networking matters: Referrals are highly valued. Always mention if you know someone at the company.

US Cover Letter Format (2026)

Here's the standard US cover letter format:

  1. Your contact information (name, email, phone, LinkedIn, location)
  2. Date
  3. Hiring manager's contact information (name, title, company, address if available)
  4. Salutation ("Dear [Hiring Manager Name],")
  5. 3-5 paragraphs (hook, experience, why company, value, closing)
  6. Sign-off ("Sincerely," or "Best regards," + your name)

What US Employers Look for in a Cover Letter

  • ✅ Clear, concise writing
  • ✅ Quantified achievements
  • ✅ Demonstrated knowledge of the company
  • ✅ Alignment with company values
  • ✅ Professionalism and attention to detail
  • ✅ Enthusiasm and genuine interest

Should You Include a Cover Letter in the US?

The answer depends on the job posting:

  • Always send one if the posting specifically asks for it.
  • Send one anyway for roles you're really excited about—it shows extra effort.
  • Skip it only if the posting clearly states "no cover letters."

Cover Letter Example for US Job Application

Subject: Application for Data Analyst – Maria Gonzalez

Dear Mr. Johnson,

I'm excited to apply for the Data Analyst position at ABC Tech, as advertised on LinkedIn. With a Bachelor's in Statistics and 4 years of experience in data analysis, I'm confident I can contribute to your data-driven decision-making.

In my current role at XYZ Corporation, I developed automated reporting dashboards using SQL and Tableau that reduced manual reporting time by 70%. I also presented weekly insights to senior leadership, helping shape business strategy.

I've been particularly impressed with ABC Tech's commitment to innovation and data ethics. Your recent expansion into machine learning-driven insights is exactly where I want to grow.

I'm eager to bring my analytical expertise and collaborative approach to ABC Tech. I look forward to discussing how I can help your team make smarter, faster decisions.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Best regards,
Maria Gonzalez

Tips for Non-US Candidates

If you're applying from outside the US, keep these tips in mind:

  • Address visa status honestly: If you require sponsorship, mention it clearly.
  • Don't be too modest: US employers value confidence. Highlight your achievements.
  • Be aware of time zones: Mention your willingness to work with US time zones if needed.
  • Keep it concise: US recruiters prefer short, punchy letters.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • ❌ Being too formal (e.g., "I hereby submit my application")
  • ❌ Using "To Whom It May Concern" without trying to find a name
  • ❌ Writing 2 pages of fluff
  • ❌ Focusing on what you want instead of what you can offer
  • ❌ Forgetting to proofread

Final Thoughts

Writing a cover letter for US jobs is about showing you're professional, confident, and value-driven. Follow the US format, tailor it to the company, and you'll be well ahead of most applicants.

FAQ

1. Do US employers read cover letters?

Yes, many do—especially for roles that require strong communication skills.

2. How long should a US cover letter be?

One page maximum, 200-350 words is standard.

3. Can I mention visa sponsorship in my cover letter?

Yes, it's honest and saves time. Be clear about your visa status.

4. Should I include a photo in my US cover letter?

No. US employers generally prefer no photos in applications.

5. Is a cover letter required for US jobs?

Not always, but it's recommended—especially for professional roles.
