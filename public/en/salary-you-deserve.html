The Salary You Deserve (According to Data)

September 18, 2026 8 min read

You have no idea what you're worth. Most people don't. And that ignorance costs you—often tens of thousands of dollars per year.

Here's how to find the salary you actually deserve, using data instead of guesswork.

Table of Contents

  • Why You're Probably Underpaid
  • Where to Find Real Salary Data
  • How to Calculate Your Fair Range
  • Factors That Affect Your Number

Why You're Probably Underpaid

Most people are underpaid. Why? Because:

  • They don't know the market rate for their role
  • They accept the first offer
  • They stay at companies too long without raises
  • They don't negotiate
  • They anchor to their current salary instead of market value

Employees who negotiate earn $1 million more over a career than those who don't.

Where to Find Real Salary Data

  • Glassdoor — company-specific salary reports
  • LinkedIn Salary — role and location benchmarks
  • Payscale — detailed compensation breakdowns
  • Levels.fyi — best for tech roles
  • BLS.gov — industry-wide wage data
  • Blind — anonymous salary sharing (especially tech)

How to Calculate Your Fair Range

  1. Find the median for your role, location, and experience level.
  2. Find the 75th percentile — this is your target.
  3. Adjust for your unique value — skills, certifications, achievements.
  4. Know your minimum — the lowest you'll accept.
  5. Ask for 10–20% above your target — leave room for negotiation.

Factors That Affect Your Number

  • Location — NYC vs. Austin vs. remote
  • Industry — tech vs. nonprofit vs. finance
  • Company size — startup vs. enterprise
  • Experience — 2 years vs. 10 years
  • Skills — AI, data, cybersecurity command premiums
  • Scarcity — how rare is your skill set?

The Gender and Negotiation Gap

Data shows persistent gaps:

  • Women earn 82–84 cents for every dollar men earn (adjusted, the gap shrinks but persists)
  • Men negotiate more often — and get better outcomes
  • Women who negotiate face more social backlash

Knowing this doesn't fix it—but it helps you prepare and push back.

How to Ask for What You Deserve

Script: "Based on my research and my experience with [specific skills], the market rate for this role is $[range]. I'm looking for a salary of $[target], which reflects my contributions and value."

Then stop talking. Let them respond.

Final Thoughts

You deserve to be paid fairly. The only way to get there is to know your value—and ask for it. Data is your leverage. Use it.

FAQ

1. How do I find the market rate for my role?

Use Glassdoor, LinkedIn Salary, Payscale, and Levels.fyi. Cross-reference multiple sources.

2. Should I tell employers my current salary?

In many US states, they can't ask. If they do, pivot to your target range based on market data.

3. How much should I ask for?

10–20% above your target, based on market research and your unique value.

4. What if they say the offer is final?

Ask about signing bonuses, equity, or performance-based raises. If it's truly final, decide based on the full package.

5. Is it worth switching companies for a raise?

Often yes—job switchers get 15–20% raises on average vs. 3–5% for staying.
