How to Prepare for a Video Interview (Complete Guide)

October 15, 2026

Video interviews are now the standard for job interviews. Whether it's Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams, you need to be prepared to make a strong impression through a screen.

In this complete guide, I'll show you how to prepare for a video interview—from technical setup to body language.

Why Video Interviews Are Different

  • Technical issues can distract
  • Your body language is more limited
  • The camera changes how you present yourself
  • There's less personal connection

Before the Interview: 10 Essential Steps

1. Test Your Technology

  • Camera: Test it works and is positioned at eye level
  • Microphone: Test sound quality, consider a headset
  • Internet: Test connection speed
  • Software: Install and test the platform (Zoom, Meet, Teams)

2. Choose the Right Location

  • Find a quiet, well-lit space
  • Ensure your background is clean and professional
  • Close doors and windows to reduce noise
  • Put your phone on silent

3. Set Up Your Lighting

Good lighting is critical. Position a light source (window or lamp) in front of you, not behind you.

4. Dress Professionally

Dress the same as you would for an in-person interview. Avoid bright patterns or neon colors.

5. Prepare Your Environment

  • Have a glass of water nearby
  • Keep your resume and notes within reach
  • Have a pen and paper ready

6. Log In Early

Log in 10-15 minutes early to test everything and calm your nerves.

7. Use the "Camera Eye" Trick

Look at the camera lens (not the screen) to simulate eye contact. This makes you appear confident and engaged.

8. Prepare Questions

Have 2-3 questions ready to ask the interviewer.

9. Do a Test Run

Practice with a friend or family member on a video call.

10. Have a Backup Plan

If your internet fails, know the interviewer's phone number. Offer to continue by phone.

During the Interview: 7 Key Tips

1. Start with a Smile

Smile when you greet the interviewer. It sets a positive tone.

2. Make Eye Contact with the Camera

Remember: the camera is your "eyes" to the interviewer. Look into it when speaking.

3. Sit Up Straight

Good posture shows confidence. Don't slouch or lean back too far.

4. Use Hand Gestures (Moderately)

Hand gestures help you communicate, but don't overdo it.

5. Speak Clearly and Slower

Audio quality can be less crisp. Speak clearly and a bit slower than normal.

6. Pause Before Answering

Take a moment to think before answering. This looks thoughtful and confident.

7. Keep Water Handy

Take a sip of water between answers if needed. It helps your voice and gives you time to think.

Post-Interview: Follow-Up

Send a thank-you email within 24 hours. Mention something specific you discussed.

Example: "Thank you for the interview today. I appreciated learning more about your team's focus on [specific topic]. I'm excited about the opportunity to contribute."

Common Video Interview Mistakes

  • ❌ No eye contact with the camera
  • ❌ Poor lighting (backlit or too dark)
  • ❌ Distractions (phone, pets, family)
  • ❌ Looking at notes constantly
  • ❌ Not testing technology first
  • ❌ Interrupting the interviewer

Final Thoughts

Video interviews are here to stay. By preparing your technology, environment, and presentation, you'll stand out to employers—even through a screen.

FAQ

1. What's the best camera angle for a video interview?

Eye level. Place your camera at eye level so you're not looking up or down.

2. Should I wear a suit for a video interview?

Yes, dress the same as you would for an in-person interview.

3. What if my internet cuts out?

Keep the interviewer's phone number handy. Offer to continue by phone.

4. Should I look at the screen or the camera?

Look at the camera lens to simulate eye contact.

5. How long should a video interview be?

Typically 30-45 minutes, but it can be longer for senior roles.
