How to Write a Short Cover Letter (That Still Gets Read)

October 10, 2026

Recruiters don't have time to read a 500-word cover letter. In fact, studies show that hiring managers spend an average of 7 seconds scanning a cover letter before deciding whether to continue reading.

That's why learning how to write a short cover letter is a superpower. A concise, punchy, and direct cover letter can get you noticed—and get you an interview.

Why Short Is Better

  • ✅ Recruiters read it in 30 seconds
  • ✅ Shows you respect their time
  • ✅ Forced to be concise and impactful
  • ✅ Easier to tailor for each job
  • ✅ Proven to get more responses

The 4-Sentence Formula for a Short Cover Letter

Here's a simple 4-sentence formula that fits in under 100 words:

  1. Sentence 1: State your interest and the role.
  2. Sentence 2: Mention your top skill and a relevant achievement.
  3. Sentence 3: Connect to the company's mission or needs.
  4. Sentence 4: Call to action + thanks.

Short Cover Letter Example (Under 100 Words)

Subject: Application for Data Analyst – John Smith

Dear Ms. Johnson,

I'm excited to apply for the Data Analyst position at TechFlow. With 4 years of experience in SQL and Python, I've built dashboards that saved my team 20 hours weekly.

TechFlow's focus on data-driven decision-making aligns perfectly with my expertise. I'm confident I can help you turn insights into action.

I'd welcome the chance to discuss how I can contribute. Thank you for your time.

Best regards,
John Smith

Another Example (50 Words)

Subject: Application for Marketing Coordinator – Maria Lopez

Dear Mr. Brown,

I'm applying for the Marketing Coordinator role at BrightWave. I've led campaigns that increased engagement by 60% and I'm passionate about data-driven marketing. I'd love to bring my skills to your team. Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,
Maria Lopez

When to Use a Short Cover Letter

  • When the job posting says "cover letter optional"
  • When you have a strong referral
  • When you're applying through an online portal with a character limit
  • When you're a senior professional with a lot of experience (your resume speaks for itself)
  • When you're emailing directly and want to be respectful of their time

When a Longer Cover Letter Is Better

  • When the job posting specifically asks for a detailed cover letter
  • When you're changing careers and need to explain your story
  • When you have a specific achievement that needs context
  • When applying for creative or writing roles

How to Write a Short Cover Letter in 5 Steps

  1. Research the company – Find 1-2 specific things you genuinely admire.
  2. Pick your best achievement – Choose the one that's most relevant to the job.
  3. Use the 4-sentence formula – Hook, skill, fit, CTA.
  4. Cut every unnecessary word – Remove "I believe," "I think," "I feel."
  5. Proofread – Send it out only when it's clean.

Common Mistakes in Short Cover Letters

  • ❌ Being so short that it's generic
  • ❌ No mention of the company
  • ❌ Too vague ("I'm a hard worker")
  • ❌ No call to action
  • ❌ Spelling errors in a short letter (they're more obvious)

Final Thoughts

Short doesn't mean weak. A well-crafted short cover letter can be more powerful than a long one because it's focused, punchy, and respectful of the recruiter's time.

FAQ

1. How short is too short for a cover letter?

Anything under 50 words is probably too short unless you have a referral. Aim for 75-150 words.

2. Can I use a short cover letter for any job?

Yes, but if the job posting specifically asks for a detailed cover letter, follow their instructions.

3. Should I send a short cover letter as a PDF?

Yes, PDF is safest to avoid formatting issues. If you're emailing, you can also paste it in the body.

4. What if I have a lot of experience to mention?

Your resume should carry the details. Use your short cover letter to highlight one or two key achievements.

5. Is a one-page cover letter considered short?

One page is standard. A "short" cover letter is typically under 150 words.
