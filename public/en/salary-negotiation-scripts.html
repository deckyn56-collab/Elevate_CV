How to Negotiate Your Salary (10 Proven Scripts)

November 27, 2026

Salary negotiation is one of the most important conversations in your career. Yet, many people are too nervous to do it—and they lose thousands of dollars over their lifetime.

In this guide, I'll show you 10 proven salary negotiation scripts that will help you negotiate with confidence in 2026.

Why You Must Negotiate

  • The first offer is rarely the best
  • Employees who negotiate earn more over time
  • Negotiating shows confidence and professionalism
  • It sets the tone for your long-term earning potential

Before You Negotiate: Do These 3 Things

  1. Research the market rate – Use sites like Glassdoor, LinkedIn Salary, and Payscale to know your worth.
  2. Know your minimum number – What's the lowest salary you'll accept?
  3. Practice your scripts out loud – Negotiation requires confidence.

10 Proven Salary Negotiation Scripts

1. The "Research-Based" Script

Best for: When you have data to support your request.

Script: "Based on my research, the market rate for this position is between $75,000 and $85,000. Given my 4 years of experience and proven track record, I believe a salary of $82,000 is justified. I'm confident this aligns with the value I bring."

2. The "Value-Based" Script

Best for: When you have quantifiable achievements.

Script: "I'm excited about this role and the opportunity to contribute. Given that I increased revenue by 30% in my last position, I'm confident I can deliver similar results here. I'm looking for a salary that reflects that value—around $85,000."

3. The "Delay" Script

Best for: When they ask about salary too early.

Script: "I appreciate you asking. Before I discuss salary, I'd love to learn more about the full scope of the role and what success looks like. Once I have a clearer picture, I'm happy to discuss compensation."

4. The "Counter-Offer" Script

Best for: When you receive a lower offer than expected.

Script: "Thank you for the offer. I'm very excited about the opportunity. I was hoping for a salary closer to $80,000 based on my research and experience. Is there flexibility in the budget to meet me at that level?"

5. The "Current Job" Script

Best for: When you're currently employed and comparing offers.

Script: "I currently earn $85,000 plus benefits. While I'm excited about this opportunity, I would need a compensation package that's competitive with my current role to justify the transition. Would you be able to match or exceed that?"

6. The "Pause" Script

Best for: When you need time to think.

Script: "Thank you for the offer. I'm genuinely excited about this opportunity. Could I have a few days to review the details and get back to you? I want to make a thoughtful decision."

7. The "Benefits" Script

Best for: When they can't increase base salary but can offer more benefits.

Script: "I understand the budget may be limited. However, if the base salary can't be increased, would you consider additional benefits like a signing bonus, more vacation days, or a flexible work schedule?"

8. The "Clear Expectations" Script

Best for: When negotiating a raise, not a new job.

Script: "I've consistently met and exceeded my performance targets this year. I'd like to discuss a raise that reflects my contributions. Could we look at a salary increase of 10%?"

9. The "Range" Script

Best for: When you're asked for your expected salary.

Script: "I'm looking for a salary in the range of $75,000 to $85,000, depending on the full scope of the role and benefits. What budget range has been set for this position?"

10. The "Confident Close" Script

Best for: When you're delivering your final number.

Script: "I really appreciate the offer. Based on my research and the value I bring, I'm looking for $85,000. I'm confident this is a fair number, and I'm excited to get started if we can align."

What to Say When They Say "No"

It's important to have a response if they can't meet your number:

Script: "I understand. Is there flexibility in other areas, like a signing bonus or additional vacation time? I really want to make this work, so I'm open to a creative solution."

Salary Negotiation Mistakes to Avoid

  • ❌ Giving a number too early
  • ❌ Accepting the first offer
  • ❌ Being apologetic about negotiating
  • ❌ Not researching market rates
  • ❌ Using a threatening tone
  • ❌ Forgetting to negotiate benefits

Final Thoughts

Negotiating your salary is a professional skill, not a confrontation. Use these scripts as a foundation, and always remember: you deserve to be paid what you're worth.

FAQ

1. When should I discuss salary in an interview?

Usually during the final round or when an offer is made. Delay if possible until you know the full scope of the role.

2. What if they ask for my current salary?

In some states, they can't ask. If they do, be honest but pivot to market rates for the role.

3. How much more should I ask for?

Ask for 10-20% more than the initial offer, but be realistic based on market research.

4. Should I negotiate benefits too?

Yes, benefits are part of your total compensation. Don't forget signing bonuses, vacation time, and flexibility.

5. What if they rescind the offer after I negotiate?

That's rare and would be a red flag. Most companies expect some negotiation and welcome it.
