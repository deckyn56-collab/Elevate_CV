How to Ask for a Raise: 10 Proven Salary Negotiation Scripts

September 5, 2026

Asking for a raise is one of the most nerve-wracking conversations you'll have in your career. But here's the truth: if you don't ask, you won't get it. In the US, employees who negotiate their salary earn significantly more over their lifetime than those who don't.

In this guide, I'll walk you through 10 proven salary negotiation scripts that will help you ask for a raise with confidence—and get results.

1. The Direct Approach

Sometimes the best approach is simple and straightforward. Be confident and direct.

Script: "I've been consistently exceeding my performance targets and contributing to key projects. I'd like to discuss adjusting my compensation to reflect my contributions."

2. The Data-Driven Approach

Use market data to support your request. This shows you've done your research.

Script: "Based on my research, the market rate for my position is between $75,000 and $85,000. Given my 3 years of experience and performance, I believe a salary of $80,000 is justified."

3. The Impact Approach

Focus on the value you've brought to the company. Quantify your achievements.

Script: "Over the last year, I've increased revenue by 20% and reduced operational costs by 15%. I'd like my compensation to reflect these contributions."

4. The Future Value Approach

Highlight your future plans and how they'll benefit the company.

Script: "Next quarter, I plan to lead a project that will increase efficiency by 30%. I'd like to discuss a raise that reflects both my current contributions and future value."

5. The Time-Based Approach

If it's been a year since your last raise, now is the perfect time to ask.

Script: "It's been a year since my last salary review. I've consistently met my goals and taken on additional responsibilities. I'd like to request a salary adjustment."

6. The Team Approach

If you're a team leader, you can highlight your leadership and team's success.

Script: "My team has consistently exceeded quarterly targets. I believe my leadership and contributions deserve recognition, and I'd like to discuss a raise."

7. The Counter-Offer Approach

If you've received another job offer, you can use it as leverage (but be careful).

Script: "I've received another offer, but I'd prefer to stay here. Can we discuss a compensation package that's competitive?"

8. The Professional Development Approach

If you've recently completed a certification or course, use it as leverage.

Script: "I've recently completed a certification that will directly impact my performance. I'd like to discuss a raise that recognizes my new skills."

9. The Soft Ask (Low Pressure)

If you're unsure about the company's financial situation, use a softer approach.

Script: "I'm really happy here and I love the work. I was wondering if there's any flexibility in my current salary."

10. The Follow-Up Approach

If your initial request was denied, don't give up. Follow up politely.

Script: "I understand that budget constraints might be a factor. Could we revisit this conversation in 3 months if my performance continues to exceed expectations?"

Summary

Asking for a raise is a necessary part of career growth. By using these 10 proven scripts, you'll be prepared to negotiate with confidence and increase your earning potential.

FAQ

1. When is the best time to ask for a raise?

The best time is during your annual performance review or after completing a significant project.

2. Should I mention a competing offer?

Only if you're genuinely willing to leave. Use it as leverage, but be careful—it can backfire.

3. What if my manager says no?

Ask for feedback and set a timeline to revisit the conversation in 3-6 months.

4. How much should I ask for?

Research market rates and aim for 10-20% above your current salary, depending on your experience.

5. Is it okay to negotiate a counter-offer?

Yes, it's normal and expected in many industries. Just be prepared to walk away if they don't meet your expectations.
