7 ATS Keywords That Will Get You Hired (2026)

August 30, 2026

Did you know that over 75% of resumes are rejected by an Applicant Tracking System (ATS) before a human recruiter even sees them? [citation:1] This automated software is the digital gatekeeper to almost every job in the US. It scans your resume for specific terms and ranks it against the job description. If your document doesn't contain the right "secret code," it’s silently discarded. To get hired in 2026, you must speak the language of the ATS: keywords [citation:15][citation:12].

In this comprehensive guide, we’ll break down the 7 key types of ATS keywords that will dramatically increase your chances of getting past the bots and into the interview room [citation:1].

Why ATS Keywords Are Crucial in 2026

ATS software is designed to filter out candidates who don't match the job requirements. It does this by parsing your resume and looking for specific keywords, phrases, and formatting. If your resume doesn't align perfectly with the job posting, the system ranks you lower, and in many cases, you’re never seen by human eyes [citation:9].

As one expert notes, the biggest mistake candidates make is using generic terms they think sound impressive. Instead, you must mirror the EXACT language used in the job description [citation:1]. This is the most critical step in beating the ATS.

How to Find Your Winning Keywords in 5 Minutes

Before we dive into the 7 types, you need to know how to find the right keywords for your specific application. Here’s a simple, 4-step process you can use for every job you apply to [citation:1]:

  1. Copy the job description into a word cloud tool (like WordClouds.com).
  2. Identify the 10 most repeated words or phrases. These are the core requirements.
  3. Check which of these words already appear in your resume.
  4. Add the missing ones with real context. Never just list them; incorporate them into your achievement bullets.

The 7 Types of ATS Keywords You Need

1. Job Title Variations

You must include the exact job title from the posting, but also common variations. For example, if the job is for a "Project Management Professional," your resume should include both that phrase and the abbreviation "PMP" [citation:4]. If the job is for a "Senior Software Engineer," you should also have "Software Developer" or "Full-Stack Engineer" if they're relevant to your experience. This ensures you match the search terms used by the ATS for both the specific role and the broader job family [citation:1].

2. Technical Skills and Hard Skills

These are the tools, technologies, and processes mentioned in the job description. For tech roles, this is critical. Don't just write "Python"; write "Developed Python scripts for data automation." If the job asks for "AWS," don't just put it in a skill list; mention "Deployed applications on AWS EC2 with Docker and Kubernetes" [citation:2][citation:7]. The keywords are the same, but providing context makes your resume more powerful for both ATS and humans [citation:13].

3. Soft Skills Mentioned in the JD

While hard skills are essential, ATS systems also scan for soft skills like "Leadership," "Communication," and "Problem-Solving." Don't just list them. Add context. For example, instead of "Strong leadership skills," write "Led a cross-functional team of 5 to achieve a 30% increase in efficiency" [citation:1]. This combines a soft skill with an action verb and a result, which is a winning combination [citation:11].

4. Industry-Specific Terminology

Every industry has its own jargon. Healthcare has "HIPAA compliance" and "EHR systems." Finance has "P&L Management" and "EBITDA." Marketing has "SEO/SEM" and "Content Strategy" [citation:9]. Using the specific terminology from your industry shows you understand the field and are not just a generalist. This is a powerful way to boost your ATS score [citation:2].

5. Action Verbs Aligned to Seniority

The verbs you use can signal your level of experience to both ATS and recruiters. Avoid weak verbs like "responsible for" or "helped with" [citation:8]. Instead, use strong, impactful verbs. For entry-level, you might use "Developed," "Assisted," "Created." For mid-level, "Led," "Managed," "Delivered." For senior roles, use "Architected," "Strategized," "Drove." These verbs are like keywords themselves and can help you rank higher [citation:1][citation:11].

6. Metrics and Numbers

ATS algorithms are increasingly "smart" and look for evidence of impact. Quantifiable achievements are the best proof of your abilities. Instead of saying "increased sales," say "increased sales by 25% in 6 months." The number "25%" is a keyword that shows a meaningful result [citation:1]. Always look for ways to quantify your past performance. This makes your resume more credible and helps you stand out from the crowd [citation:11].

7. Company Size/Scope Indicators

Finally, include terms that describe the scope of your experience. If you managed a project for a "global" company or "enterprise" client, use those words. If you worked in a "startup" or "SaaS" environment, mention it. Terms like "cross-functional," "global team," "enterprise," and "large-scale" are often in job descriptions and act as powerful keywords [citation:1].

How to Use Keywords Without "Stuffing"

One of the biggest mistakes candidates make is keyword stuffing—repeating terms in a way that sounds unnatural. For example: "Project Management. Agile. Leadership. Data. Python. SQL." This is a red flag for both ATS and human reviewers [citation:4][citation:12].

The right way is to embed keywords in sentences that describe your actions and results. For example [citation:4]:

"Led Agile sprint planning for a 12-person team, improving data analysis pipeline throughput by 40% using Python and SQL."

This sentence contains multiple keywords (Agile, sprint planning, data analysis, Python, SQL) but sounds completely natural and demonstrates your expertise. This is the "magic formula": Action + What You Did + Result with the right keywords sprinkled in [citation:12].

Final Thoughts

To get your resume past the ATS in 2026, you must be strategic. Don't just list your skills; know the secret keywords from the job description and weave them into your achievement statements [citation:15].

FAQ

1. What are ATS keywords?

ATS keywords are the specific terms, skills, job titles, and certifications from a job description that an applicant tracking system scans for to filter and rank candidates [citation:4].

2. How many ATS keywords should I include?

There is no strict number, but include 10-15 relevant keywords naturally. The goal is to hit a high match rate with the job description, not to stuff as many as possible [citation:4][citation:14].

3. Should I use action verbs as keywords?

Yes. Strong action verbs like "Led," "Delivered," and "Architected" are powerful because they signal your level of experience and responsibility [citation:1][citation:8].

4. What is the best file format for ATS?

Use a simple, machine-readable format like .docx or a text-based PDF. This ensures the ATS can parse all your keywords correctly [citation:3][citation:14].

5. How do I find the right keywords?

Copy the job description into a word cloud tool or simply read it carefully. Look for repeated words and phrases, which are the most important keywords [citation:1][citation:15].
