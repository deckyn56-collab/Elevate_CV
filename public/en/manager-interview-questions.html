10 Interview Questions for Managers (and How to Answer)

November 9, 2026

Interviewing for a management role requires more than just technical skills. You need to demonstrate leadership, emotional intelligence, and the ability to drive results through your team.

In this guide, I'll show you the 10 most common manager interview questions and how to answer them confidently.

1. "How Do You Handle Conflict Within Your Team?"

Why they ask: To see if you can manage difficult situations calmly and effectively.

Best approach: Show that you address conflict directly, listen to all sides, and focus on solutions.

Example: "I address conflict directly but respectfully. I schedule a one-on-one with each person to understand their perspective, then bring them together to find a solution. In my last role, I mediated a dispute between two team members by focusing on common goals. They became more collaborative afterward."

2. "How Do You Motivate Your Team?"

Why they ask: To see if you understand what drives people.

Best approach: Show you understand individual motivations and use a mix of recognition, growth opportunities, and autonomy.

Example: "I believe in understanding what drives each person. Some want career growth, others want recognition, and some want autonomy. I take time to learn this and adapt my approach. I also make sure to celebrate wins publicly and provide regular feedback."

3. "Tell Me About a Time You Made a Difficult Decision."

Why they ask: To assess your judgment and decision-making skills.

Best approach: Use the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result).

Example: "In a previous role, I had to decide whether to kill a project that the team was passionate about. I analyzed the data and realized it wasn't viable. I made the difficult call to stop it, communicated clearly, and redirected the team to a more promising project. That project ended up generating 30% more revenue."

4. "How Do You Prioritize Tasks When Everything Feels Urgent?"

Why they ask: To test your time management and strategic thinking.

Best approach: Show you use frameworks (Eisenhower Matrix, ABC prioritization) and communicate with stakeholders.

Example: "I use a prioritization framework like the Eisenhower Matrix to distinguish between urgent and important tasks. I also communicate with stakeholders to align on expectations. If there's genuinely a conflict, I'm transparent about it and ask for guidance."

5. "How Do You Handle Underperforming Team Members?"

Why they ask: To see if you can manage performance issues directly and fairly.

Best approach: Show you understand the root cause first, then offer support, and escalate if necessary.

Example: "I first have a one-on-one conversation to understand the root cause—it could be lack of resources, personal issues, or unclear expectations. I then create a performance improvement plan with specific goals and support. If there's no improvement, I escalate according to company policy."

6. "What's Your Leadership Style?"

Why they ask: To see if your style is a fit for the team and culture.

Best approach: Be honest but flexible. Show you adapt to the situation and the team's needs.

Example: "I'd describe my style as collaborative. I believe in involving the team in decisions and fostering open communication. But I'm also not afraid to make tough calls when needed. I adapt my style based on the situation—more directive during crises, more supportive during development."

7. "How Do You Handle Stress and Pressure?"

Why they ask: To test your emotional resilience.

Best approach: Show you have practical strategies (organization, delegation, mindfulness).

Example: "I handle stress by staying organized and focusing on what I can control. I also make sure to delegate appropriately and communicate openly with my team. During a recent product launch, I managed tight deadlines by breaking tasks into smaller steps and checking in with the team daily."

8. "Tell Me About a Time You Failed as a Manager."

Why they ask: To test accountability and learning ability.

Best approach: Be honest, show what you learned, and how you changed.

Example: "Early in my management career, I was too hands-off with a team member. They ended up missing a critical deadline. I took responsibility for not providing enough support. I learned the importance of regular check-ins. Now I have a more structured approach to communication."

9. "What Sets You Apart from Other Managers?"

Why they ask: To understand your unique value proposition.

Best approach: Highlight a unique strength or combination of strengths.

Example: "My combination of technical expertise and emotional intelligence sets me apart. I can dive deep into the technical aspects of a project while also building strong relationships with the team. This enables me to make decisions that are both data-driven and people-focused."

10. "How Would You Handle a Situation Where a Team Member Is Resisting Change?"

Why they ask: To see how you manage resistance and build buy-in.

Best approach: Listen to their concerns, explain the "why," and involve them in the process.

Example: "I start by listening to their concerns to understand why they're resistant. It might be fear of the unknown or a genuine concern about the impact. I explain the 'why' behind the change and try to involve them in the process. In a recent project, I asked a resistant team member to be part of the planning group. They became one of the change champions."

Bonus Questions to Prepare For

  • How do you build trust with your team?
  • How do you give difficult feedback?
  • What's your process for hiring?
  • How do you handle budget constraints?
  • How do you develop your team's skills?

The STAR Method for Manager Interviews

For behavioral questions, use the STAR method:

Component What to Say
Situation Set the scene (context)
Task What was your responsibility?
Action What steps did you take as a manager?
Result What was the outcome? (with numbers if possible)

Final Thoughts

Interviewing for a management role is about showing you can lead people, make tough decisions, and drive results. Use these questions as a guide, and always back up your answers with real examples.

FAQ

1. What are the most common manager interview questions?

Questions about conflict management, motivation, decision-making, and team development are most common.

2. How do I prepare for a manager interview?

Review your past leadership experiences, practice behavioral answers using the STAR method, and research the company's culture.

3. How do I answer "What's your leadership style?"

Be honest and show you adapt. Mention collaborative, supportive, and decisive approaches with examples.

4. How do I handle the "failure" question?

Be honest, show accountability, and focus on what you learned and how you improved.

5. What should I ask the interviewer?

Ask about team structure, leadership expectations, company culture, and growth opportunities.
