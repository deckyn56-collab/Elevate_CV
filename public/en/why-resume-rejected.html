Why Your Resume Gets Rejected (And How to Fix It)

September 18, 2026 9 min read

You've sent out hundreds of applications. Silence. Or the dreaded "we've decided to move forward with other candidates." Why?

The reasons are rarely what you think. Here are the real reasons resumes get rejected—and exactly how to fix them.

1. The ATS Filter

75% of resumes are rejected by Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) before a human sees them. If your resume isn't optimized for ATS, it doesn't matter how good you are.

The fix: Use a simple, single-column layout. No tables, graphics, or fancy fonts.

2. Missing Keywords

ATS systems scan for specific keywords from the job description. If you don't have them, you're ranked low or auto-rejected.

The fix: Mirror the exact language from the job posting. If they say "project management," use that phrase—not "managed projects."

3. Format Kills Readability

Recruiters spend 7 seconds on initial scan. If your resume is cluttered, dense, or hard to navigate, they skip it.

The fix: Use clear headings, bullet points, and white space. Make it easy to scan.

4. Vague, Generic Bullets

"Responsible for marketing" says nothing. "Helped with sales" says nothing. Generic bullets signal generic work.

The fix: Use strong action verbs and specific descriptions. "Led SEO strategy that increased organic traffic by 80%."

5. No Numbers, No Impact

Without metrics, your achievements are unverifiable and forgettable. Numbers stand out visually and prove impact.

The fix: Quantify everything. Revenue, savings, percentages, team sizes, timeframes.

6. Too Long or Too Short

5 pages = "can't prioritize." Half a page = "not enough experience." Both get rejected.

The fix: 1 page for <10 years experience. 2 pages max for senior roles.

7. Typos and Grammar Errors

This is the #1 killer. One typo signals carelessness and lack of attention to detail.

The fix: Proofread 3 times. Have a friend review it. Use Grammarly.

8. Not Tailored to the Job

Sending the same generic resume to 100 jobs is the fastest way to get ignored. It shows you didn't put in effort.

The fix: Spend 15–20 minutes customizing for each application. Highlight what's relevant to that role.

How to Fix Your Resume

  1. Run it through an ATS checker. Tools like Jobscan help.
  2. Add keywords from the job description.
  3. Simplify the format — single column, standard fonts.
  4. Quantify every bullet. No numbers = no credibility.
  5. Keep it to 1–2 pages. Every line must earn its place.
  6. Proofread 3 times. Then have someone else read it.
  7. Tailor for each application. 15 minutes of customization beats 100 generic sends.

Final Thoughts

Your resume isn't a history document—it's a marketing document. Fix these 8 issues, and you'll dramatically increase your chances of getting interviews.

FAQ

1. How do I know if ATS rejected my resume?

You usually don't. Use an ATS checker like Jobscan to see how your resume scores against the job description.

2. Should I include a photo?

No. US employers prefer no photos, and they can hurt more than help.

3. How long should my resume be?

1 page for <10 years experience, 2 pages max for senior roles.

4. How many keywords should I include?

10–15 relevant keywords from the job description, used naturally.

5. How often should I update my resume?

Every few months, or whenever you complete a significant project.
