How to Deal with a Difficult Coworker (2026)

September 19, 2026 8 min read

You can't choose your coworkers. And sometimes, you get stuck with someone who makes every interaction exhausting—passive-aggressive, credit-stealing, or just plain difficult.

Here's how to deal with a difficult coworker without losing your cool or your career.

4 Types of Difficult Coworkers

  • The Credit Thief — takes credit for your work
  • The Passive-Aggressive — snide comments and subtle digs
  • The Drama Magnet — turns everything into a crisis
  • The Slacker — doesn't pull their weight, blames others

Rule 1: Don't Take the Bait

Difficult people want a reaction. Don't give them one. Stay calm, neutral, and professional. If they make a snide comment, respond with a simple question or a bland statement—nothing emotional.

Rule 2: Document Everything

Keep a written record of interactions—emails, missed deadlines, credit-stealing incidents. Include dates, times, and specific examples. This protects you if you need to escalate.

Rule 3: Keep It Professional

Don't gossip about them, don't stoop to their level, don't complain to everyone. Stay above the fray. Being the calm one in the room is a power move.

Rule 4: Address It Directly (Once)

Sometimes a difficult person doesn't realize how they're coming across. If you're comfortable, have a direct conversation:

"Hey, I noticed something in our last meeting. I felt like [specific issue]. Can we talk about how we can work together better?"

Do this once, privately. If nothing changes, escalate or distance yourself.

Rule 5: Escalate Carefully

If the behavior continues and affects your work, speak to your manager. Bring documentation, frame it as "improving team dynamics" not "attacking them." Focus on facts, not feelings.

When to Leave

Sometimes, the difficult coworker is a symptom of a toxic workplace. If you've tried everything and nothing helps—or if the stress is affecting your health—it might be time to leave.

Final Thoughts

You can't fix difficult people. But you can control how you respond. Stay calm, document, keep it professional, and know when to walk away.

FAQ

1. Should I confront a difficult coworker?

Once, privately, respectfully. If nothing changes, stop confronting and start documenting.

2. When should I go to my manager?

When the behavior affects your work, your team, or your mental health—and after you've documented it.

3. Should I tell HR?

Only if the behavior crosses into harassment, discrimination, or policy violations.

4. What if my coworker is my boss's favorite?

Focus on your own performance and documentation. Don't engage in politics.

5. Is it worth quitting over?

Only if the stress is affecting your health or career. Otherwise, distance yourself and stay professional.
