How to Stop Comparing Yourself to Others (2026)

September 19, 2026 7 min read

Social media, LinkedIn, and even casual conversations constantly show us how others are doing—and it's easy to feel like you're falling behind. Comparison is natural, but it's also a trap.

Here's how to stop comparing yourself to others—and focus on your own path.

Why We Compare

  • Evolutionary: comparison helped us survive in groups
  • Social: we learn norms by watching others
  • Psychological: we measure self-worth by external markers

Comparison isn't inherently bad—but constant, toxic comparison is.

The Harm of Comparison

  • Anxiety and depression
  • Imposter syndrome
  • Lower self-esteem
  • Poor decisions (chasing others' goals)
  • Stalled progress (paralysis)

Fix 1: Limit Social Media

Social media is engineered to trigger comparison. Set limits:

  • No social media before 10 AM
  • Unfollow accounts that make you feel bad
  • Use app timers
  • Take weekends off

Fix 2: Compare to Past You

Instead of comparing yourself to others, compare yourself to who you were a year ago. Are you better, wiser, more capable? That's the real metric.

Fix 3: Define Your Own Success

Write down your definition of success—without thinking about anyone else's. Include:

  • What kind of work you want
  • How much money you need (not want)
  • What relationships matter
  • How you want to spend your time
  • What kind of person you want to be

Fix 4: Practice Gratitude

Gratitude is the antidote to comparison. Every night, write down 3 things you're grateful for. Research shows it reduces envy and increases contentment.

Fix 5: Focus on Your Strengths

Identify your top strengths and invest in them. When you focus on what you're good at, others' success stops feeling like a threat.

Fix 6: Use Comparison as Information

Sometimes comparison can be useful—if it teaches you something. Ask:

  • What did they do that worked?
  • What can I learn from their path?
  • What's transferable to my situation?

Use comparison as data, not as self-judgment.

Final Thoughts

You will always find someone doing better—or worse—than you. The goal isn't to stop noticing, but to stop judging. Focus on your own path, and comparison loses its power.

FAQ

1. Is comparison always bad?

No—used as information, it can motivate you. It's toxic when it becomes constant self-judgment.

2. How do I stop comparing on LinkedIn?

Limit scroll time, unfollow accounts that drain you, and remember: everyone posts highlights, not reality.

3. What if I keep comparing myself?

Notice it. Don't judge yourself for it. Then redirect: "What's one thing I can do today?"

4. Does comparison ever help?

Yes—when it teaches you something. Study what works for others, then adapt it.

5. How long does it take to stop?

It's ongoing. But with practice, comparison stops driving your mood and decisions.
