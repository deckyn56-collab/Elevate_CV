How to End a Cover Letter (With 10 Professional Closings)

October 16, 2026

The last sentence of your cover letter is just as important as the first. It's your final impression—the last thing a recruiter reads before deciding whether to contact you.

In this guide, I'll show you how to end a cover letter with confidence, plus 10 professional closings you can use for any job application.

The 3-Part Structure of a Strong Closing

  1. Call to Action (CTA) – What you want to happen next
  2. Appreciation – Thank them for their time
  3. Professional Sign-off – Formal but friendly closing

10 Professional Cover Letter Closings

1. The Direct CTA Closing

Use when: You want to sound confident and proactive.

Example: "I'd love the opportunity to discuss how my experience aligns with your goals. When can we set up a call?"

2. The Thank You Closing

Use when: You want to show gratitude and professionalism.

Example: "Thank you for considering my application. I appreciate your time and look forward to hearing from you."

3. The Enthusiastic Closing

Use when: You're genuinely excited about the role.

Example: "I'm truly excited about the possibility of joining your team and contributing to your success. I look forward to the next steps!"

4. The Eager to Discuss Closing

Use when: You want to invite further conversation.

Example: "I'm eager to discuss how my background and skills can benefit your team. Please feel free to reach out at your earliest convenience."

5. The "Available at Your Convenience" Closing

Use when: You want to be flexible and accommodating.

Example: "I am available for an interview at your convenience and would be happy to provide any additional information you might need."

6. The "I Look Forward to" Closing

Use when: You want to sound professional and optimistic.

Example: "I look forward to the possibility of speaking with you and learning more about how I can contribute to your team."

7. The "Thank You + CTA" Closing (Most Common)

Use when: You want a balanced, professional approach.

Example: "Thank you for your time and consideration. I would love to discuss my application further and explore how I can add value to your organization."

8. The "Hope to Connect" Closing

Use when: You want to sound approachable and warm.

Example: "I hope to connect with you soon to discuss how I can contribute to your team's success. Thank you for your consideration."

9. The "Do Not Hesitate" Closing

Use when: You want to invite questions.

Example: "Please do not hesitate to contact me if you require any further information or documentation. I'm happy to provide whatever is needed."

10. The "Warm Regards" Closing

Use when: You want a friendly but professional tone.

Example: "I appreciate your time and consideration, and I hope to hear from you soon. Warm regards."

Sign-Off Options (What to Write Before Your Name)

Choose one of these professional sign-offs based on the tone you want:

Sign-off Best for
Sincerely, Formal applications (finance, law, corporate)
Best regards, Professional and friendly (most roles)
Yours faithfully, Very formal (UK style, traditional)
Thank you, When you want to emphasize gratitude
Warm regards, Friendly but still professional
Kind regards, Polite and approachable
Best, Short, modern, and professional
Yours truly, Traditional and formal
Respectfully, Formal and respectful (especially for government roles)
With appreciation, Warm and grateful

What NOT to Write in a Closing

  • ❌ "I hope you consider my application" (passive)
  • ❌ "Please contact me if needed" (too vague)
  • ❌ "I can start whenever you want" (desperate)
  • ❌ "Thanks and I hope to hear back soon" (too casual)
  • ❌ "Yours truly" (too old-fashioned for most industries)

Full Example with Strong Closing

Dear Ms. Johnson,

Thank you for considering my application for the Marketing Manager role. I'm excited about the opportunity to bring my experience in data-driven campaigns to your team.

I would love the chance to discuss how my background can contribute to BrightWave's success. I'm available at your convenience for an interview.

Best regards,
Sarah Chen

Final Thoughts

A strong closing can leave a lasting impression. Use one of these 10 professional closings, pair it with an appropriate sign-off, and you'll end your cover letter on a high note.

FAQ

1. What is the best way to end a cover letter?

Use a clear call to action, express gratitude, and end with a professional sign-off like "Best regards" or "Sincerely."

2. Should I say "thank you" at the end of a cover letter?

Yes, thanking the recruiter is professional and polite. It shows respect for their time.

3. Can I use "Best" as a sign-off?

Yes, "Best" or "Best regards" are both professional and widely accepted in modern job applications.

4. How long should my closing paragraph be?

2-3 sentences is ideal. Keep it brief and confident.

5. Should I mention a call to action?

Yes, a specific call to action (like requesting an interview) shows confidence and initiative.
