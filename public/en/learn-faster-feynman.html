How to Learn Anything Faster (The Feynman Technique)

September 17, 2026 7 min read

Richard Feynman was one of the greatest physicists of the 20th century — and one of the best teachers. His secret wasn't memorization. It was simplification.

The Feynman Technique is a 4-step learning method that helps you understand anything deeply in less time. Here's how to use it.

Why Most Learning Fails

Most people "learn" by highlighting, re-reading, and watching videos. This feels productive, but it's shallow. You recognize the material without understanding it.

The Feynman Technique forces you to confront what you don't know — and that's where real learning happens.

Step 1: Pick a Concept

Choose one concept you want to learn. Write it at the top of a blank page.

Examples: "How compound interest works." "What is machine learning?" "The basics of copywriting."

Step 2: Explain It to a Child

Write an explanation of the concept as if you're teaching it to a 12-year-old. Use plain language. No jargon. Short sentences.

If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it yet. This step exposes every weak spot in your knowledge.

Step 3: Identify Gaps

Read what you wrote. Where did you get stuck? Where did you use jargon to hide confusion? Where did you run out of words?

Those gaps are your curriculum. Go back to the source material — book, course, article — and fill only those gaps.

Step 4: Refine and Simplify

Rewrite your explanation. Cleaner. Simpler. Shorter. Use analogies and examples.

Repeat Steps 2–4 until your explanation is so clear that a 12-year-old could understand it.

Example in Action

Concept: Compound interest.

First attempt: "Compound interest is when your interest earns interest. It grows exponentially over time."

Problem: What does "exponentially" mean? A 12-year-old wouldn't get it.

Second attempt: "Imagine you have $100. You earn $10 interest. Now you have $110. Next year, you earn 10% on the $110 — so $11. You're earning interest on your interest."

Result: Clear, concrete, memorable.

Tips for Faster Learning

  • Teach others. Explaining forces clarity.
  • Use spaced repetition. Review at increasing intervals.
  • Practice retrieval. Test yourself — don't just re-read.
  • Use analogies. Connect new ideas to things you already know.
  • Focus on one thing at a time. Deep beats shallow.
  • Sleep on it. Memory consolidates during sleep.

Final Thoughts

You can use the Feynman Technique to learn coding, marketing, finance, or anything else. It's not just faster — it's deeper. And the ability to explain things simply is a superpower in any career.

FAQ

1. What is the Feynman Technique?

A 4-step method: pick a concept, explain it simply, identify gaps, refine the explanation.

2. Why does it work?

Because explaining forces you to confront what you don't actually understand.

3. Can I use it for any subject?

Yes — coding, marketing, finance, science, languages, anything.

4. How long does it take?

15–30 minutes per concept. Deep understanding beats shallow repetition.

5. Should I write it or say it?

Both work. Writing slows you down and reveals gaps faster.
