How to Delete Your Facebook Account Permanently (2026 Guide)

September 14, 2026

Facebook has been part of our lives for over a decade. But many people are now choosing to walk away — for privacy, security, or a digital detox. The problem? Deleting Facebook isn't as simple as it sounds. There's a critical difference between deactivating and permanently deleting — plus data that may remain even after deletion.

This guide walks you through the official process and the risks you should know.

Deactivate vs Delete: The Critical Difference

Steps to Permanently Delete Facebook

Step 1: Log In to Facebook

Open the app or browser and log in to the account you want to delete.

Step 2: Open Settings

Click your profile photo in the top right, then select Settings & Privacy → Settings.

Step 3: Open Accounts Center

Click Accounts Center on the left sidebar (browser) or menu (app).

Step 4: Go to Personal Details

Select Personal Details, then Account Ownership and Control.

Step 5: Choose Deletion

Select Deactivation or Deletion, choose the account, then click Delete Account and Continue.

Step 6: Confirm

Enter your password to confirm, then click Delete Account.

The 30-Day Grace Period

Facebook gives you 30 days. If you change your mind, simply log in during that period to cancel deletion. After 30 days, the account and all data are permanently erased and cannot be recovered.

The Shadow Profile Problem

This is what most people don't know. Even after you delete your account, Meta may retain your data for up to 180 days.

Beyond that, there's something called a "shadow profile" — a hidden profile containing your information (name, phone number, email) that Meta collects from other users' contact lists, even if you never had a Facebook account.

You can request removal of this shadow data through Meta's dedicated page: facebook.com/contacts/removal.
