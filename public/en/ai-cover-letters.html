AI Cover Letters: Do They Work?

September 18, 2026 7 min read

Cover letters are the most hated part of job applications—and the easiest to automate. But do AI-generated cover letters actually work? Or do recruiters see right through them?

The answer is complicated. Here's what we know.

Table of Contents

  • The Verdict
  • What Recruiters Say
  • When AI Cover Letters Work
  • When They Fail
  • How to Use AI Effectively
  • Prompts That Work

The Verdict

AI cover letters work when:

  • They're used as a starting point
  • They're heavily edited to sound like you
  • They include specific details about the company and role

AI cover letters fail when:

  • They're sent without editing
  • They sound generic and templated
  • They don't mention the company or role by name

What Recruiters Say

In interviews, recruiters told us:

  • "I can spot AI cover letters in 3 seconds."
  • "If it doesn't mention anything about our company, it's a no."
  • "I'd rather read a short, real cover letter than a long, fake one."

So yes—recruiters are getting good at spotting AI. Editing is essential.

When AI Cover Letters Work

  • You have no time. AI gives you a first draft in seconds.
  • You struggle with structure. AI can organize your thoughts.
  • You're multilingual. AI helps you write in fluent English.
  • You're drafting, not sending. AI is a tool, not a final product.

When They Fail

  • You send it raw. Copy-paste is obvious.
  • No specifics. "I'm excited about your company" tells nothing.
  • Generic praise. "You're a leader in the industry" is meaningless.
  • Wrong tone. AI defaults to formal, corporate-sounding language.

How to Use AI Effectively

  1. Use AI for outlines. Ask for a 3-paragraph structure.
  2. Write the personal stories yourself. AI can't have your experiences.
  3. Add specific company details. Mention recent news, products, or values.
  4. Edit heavily. Rewrite at least 50% in your own words.
  5. Read it out loud. If it doesn't sound like you, rewrite.

Prompts That Work

  • "Give me a 3-paragraph outline for a cover letter to [company] for [role]. Leave placeholders for my personal stories."
  • "Here's my draft: [paste]. Rewrite it to be 30% shorter and more conversational."
  • "List 3 specific things about [company] I should mention in my cover letter."
  • "Rewrite this opening line to be less generic: [paste]."

Final Thoughts

AI cover letters work—if you use them as a tool, not a replacement. Recruiters want to hear you. Let AI help you organize and polish, but keep your voice at the center.

FAQ

1. Do recruiters hate AI cover letters?

They hate unedited AI cover letters. Edited, personal ones are fine.

2. Should I disclose I used AI?

Not necessary—if the final letter sounds like you.

3. What's the best AI for cover letters?

ChatGPT or Claude for drafting; LamaranAI for tailored output.

4. How long should an AI-assisted cover letter be?

200–350 words, or one page max. Shorter is better.

5. Can AI write a cover letter for a career change?

Yes—it can help frame transferable skills. But the personal story must be yours.
