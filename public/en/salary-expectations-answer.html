How to Answer "What Are Your Salary Expectations?" (2026 Guide)

September 8, 2026

"What are your salary expectations?" It's one of the most nerve-wracking questions in any interview. Answer too high, and you might price yourself out. Answer too low, and you might leave money on the table.

In this guide, I'll show you proven strategies to answer the salary question with confidence—and get the compensation you deserve.

Why Employers Ask This Question

  • To check if you're within their budget
  • To see if you're realistic about your worth
  • To filter candidates early in the process
  • To make an offer they think you'll accept

What NOT to Say

  • ❌ "I'll take anything" (desperate, no value)
  • ❌ "What are you offering?" (sounds evasive)
  • ❌ "I don't know" (unprepared)
  • ❌ "I need at least $100K" (too rigid)
  • ❌ "I'll let you decide" (passive)

The 3 Best Strategies (Pick One)

Strategy 1: The "Range" Approach (Safest)

How it works: Give a range based on research, and emphasize flexibility.

Example: "I'm looking for a salary in the range of $75,000 to $85,000, depending on the full scope of the role and benefits. I'm flexible and open to discussing the full package."

Strategy 2: The "Pivot" Approach (Best for Early Interviews)

How it works: Pivot the question back to them to learn more about the role.

Example: "I'd love to learn more about the full scope of the role first. Could you share the budget range that has been set for this position?"

Strategy 3: The "Research-Based" Approach (Most Confident)

How it works: Reference market research to justify your number.

Example: "Based on my research, the market rate for this role in this location is between $80,000 and $90,000. Given my experience and track record, I believe $85,000 is a fair starting point."

How to Prepare Before the Interview

  1. Research the market rate – Use Glassdoor, LinkedIn Salary, Payscale.
  2. Know your minimum number – The lowest you'll accept.
  3. Know your target number – What you want to get.
  4. Prepare a range – e.g., $75K-$85K, with $80K as your target.
  5. Practice out loud – It should sound confident, not rehearsed.

How to Handle Different Scenarios

Scenario 1: They Ask Early in the Interview

Best response: Pivot to learn more about the role first.

Example: "Before I share a number, I'd love to understand the full scope of the role and the team's goals. Could you walk me through what success looks like?"

Scenario 2: They Ask During the Offer Stage

Best response: Give a clear, confident number with research.

Example: "Thank you for the offer. Based on my research and the value I bring, I'm looking for $85,000. I'm confident this is a fair number, and I'm excited to get started if we can align."

Scenario 3: They Push for a Specific Number

Best response: Give a range with a clear justification.

Example: "I'm looking for a salary in the range of $80,000 to $85,000. I've researched the market, and this reflects my 4 years of experience and proven track record."

5 Complete Answer Examples

Example 1: Mid-Level Professional

Answer: "Based on my research and 4 years of experience in data analytics, I'm looking for a salary in the range of $75,000 to $85,000. I'm flexible and open to discussing the full compensation package."

Example 2: Senior Professional

Answer: "I'm looking for a salary in the range of $90,000 to $100,000. This is based on my 8 years of experience in project management and the market rate for this role."

Example 3: Fresh Graduate

Answer: "I'm looking for a salary in the range of $50,000 to $60,000. I'm open to discussing the full package, including benefits and growth opportunities."

Example 4: Career Changer

Answer: "I'm looking for a salary in the range of $65,000 to $75,000. I bring transferable skills from my previous career, and I'm confident I can contribute quickly."

Example 5: Remote Role

Answer: "I'm looking for a salary in the range of $70,000 to $80,000, depending on the location and benefits. I've researched the market for remote roles in this industry."

What If They Say "That's Above Our Budget"?

Best response: Ask about flexibility in other areas.

Example: "I understand. Is there flexibility in other areas like a signing bonus, additional vacation days, or a higher performance bonus structure? I'm open to a creative solution."

Final Thoughts

Answering the salary question doesn't have to be scary. Use one of these strategies, prepare your numbers, and you'll negotiate with confidence.

FAQ

1. Should I give a range or a specific number?

A range is safer. It shows flexibility while giving you a ceiling.

2. Should I ask for more than I expect?

Yes, but stay realistic. Ask 10-20% above your target to leave room for negotiation.

3. What if I don't know the market rate?

Research it! Use Glassdoor, LinkedIn Salary, or Payscale before your interview.

4. Is it okay to pivot the question back to them?

Yes, especially if they ask too early. It's a professional and strategic move.

5. What if they refuse to give a range?

Give your range confidently and ask for feedback.
