10 Things You Should Never Say in an Interview (2026)

September 18, 2026 7 min read

Interviews are won and lost on small phrases. One wrong sentence can undo an otherwise great conversation. Here are 10 things you should never say in an interview—and what to say instead.

Table of Contents

  • 1. "I'm a perfectionist."
  • 2. "What does your company do?"
  • 3. "I hated my last boss."
  • 4. "I don't have any weaknesses."
  • 5. "How much vacation do I get?"
  • 6. "I'll take anything."
  • 7. "I'm nervous."
  • 8. "What's the salary?" (too early)
  • 9. "I don't have any questions."
  • 10. "I think I'll be bored."
  • What to Say Instead

1. "I'm a perfectionist."

This is the most overused fake weakness in interview history. Interviewers hear it as "I'm avoiding the question."

Instead: Name a real weakness and show how you're improving it. "I used to struggle with public speaking. I joined Toastmasters and now present to 50+ people regularly."

2. "What does your company do?"

This signals you didn't research the company—instant red flag.

Instead: Show you've done your homework. "I've followed your expansion into the European market, and I'm curious about the strategy behind it."

3. "I hated my last boss."

Criticizing a former employer makes interviewers wonder what you'll say about them.

Instead: Stay positive. "I learned a lot there, but I'm looking for a role with more growth opportunities."

4. "I don't have any weaknesses."

This signals a lack of self-awareness. Everyone has weaknesses.

Instead: Name one real weakness + a concrete improvement plan.

5. "How much vacation do I get?"

Asking about vacation or benefits in the first interview signals you're focused on perks, not the role.

Instead: Save benefits questions for the offer stage.

6. "I'll take anything."

This sounds desperate and vague. It doesn't show you value your skills.

Instead: Be specific about what you're looking for and why this role fits.

7. "I'm nervous."

Some interviewers accept this. But it can signal lack of confidence—which matters in client-facing roles.

Instead: Breathe. Speak slowly. Confidence is a skill you can practice.

8. "What's the salary?" (too early)

Raising salary in the first interview signals you care more about money than the work.

Instead: Delay salary talk until you understand the role. If asked early, pivot: "I'd love to learn more about the role first."

9. "I don't have any questions."

This signals disinterest. Always have 2-3 questions ready.

Instead: "Yes, I'd love to know what success looks like in the first 6 months."

10. "I think I'll be bored."

Even if the role is less challenging than you'd like, saying "bored" is toxic.

Instead: Focus on growth. "I'm looking for a role where I can learn and contribute."

What to Say Instead

  • Research the company before every interview.
  • Name real weaknesses + improvement plan.
  • Stay positive about past employers.
  • Delay salary and benefits talk until the offer stage.
  • Prepare 2-3 questions for the interviewer.
  • Be specific about why you want this role.

Final Thoughts

Interviews are conversations—but they're conversations with rules. Avoid these phrases, focus on clarity and positivity, and you'll stand out for the right reasons.

FAQ

1. Is it ever okay to say "I'm a perfectionist"?

No. It's overused and sounds fake. Name a real weakness instead.

2. What if the interviewer asks about salary in the first interview?

Pivot: "I'd love to learn more about the role first. Could you share the budget range?"

3. Should I be honest about hating my last boss?

No. Stay positive. Focus on what you're looking for, not what you're leaving.

4. How many questions should I ask?

2-3 questions that show you've researched the company and care about the role.

5. What if I'm actually nervous?

Practice out loud. Record yourself. Confidence comes from preparation.
