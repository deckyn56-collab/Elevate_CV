10 LinkedIn Headlines That Get You Noticed (2026)

September 12, 2026

Your LinkedIn headline is the first thing recruiters see when they find you in search results. With over 1 billion users on LinkedIn, you need to stand out.

In this guide, I'll show you 10 LinkedIn headline formulas and examples that get you noticed by recruiters in 2026.

Why Your LinkedIn Headline Matters

  • It appears in search results, connection requests, and your profile
  • It's the most searched element of your profile
  • It's your first impression to recruiters
  • It can make you discoverable (or invisible) in LinkedIn search

The 4 Types of LinkedIn Headlines

1. The "Job Title + Keywords" Headline

Best for: Most professionals, especially job seekers.

Formula: [Job Title] | [Key Skill 1] | [Key Skill 2] | [Experience Level]

2. The "Value Proposition" Headline

Best for: Executives, freelancers, and consultants.

Formula: [What You Do] + [Who You Help] + [Result You Deliver]

3. The "Creative" Headline

Best for: Creatives, designers, writers, and storytellers.

Formula: [Creative phrase] + [What You Do] + [Personality]

4. The "Mix" Headline

Best for: Career changers, students, and professionals with unique backgrounds.

Formula: [Your Story] + [Your Skills] + [Your Direction]

10 LinkedIn Headline Examples

1. The "Job Title + Keywords" Headline (Marketing)

Example: "Marketing Manager | B2B Growth | SEO & Content Strategy | 5+ Years Experience"

2. The "Job Title + Keywords" Headline (Tech)

Example: "Software Engineer | React & Node.js | Building Scalable Products | 4+ Years Experience"

3. The "Value Proposition" Headline (Executive)

Example: "VP of Operations | Scaling Startups from 10 to 100 Employees | Driving Efficiency & Growth"

4. The "Value Proposition" Headline (Freelancer)

Example: "Freelance Copywriter | Helping B2B Brands Tell Better Stories | 50+ Happy Clients"

5. The "Creative" Headline (Designer)

Example: "Designing Brands That People Love | Creative Director | Visual Storyteller"

6. The "Creative" Headline (Writer)

Example: "Words That Move People | Content Strategist | Storyteller | Clean Copy"

7. The "Mix" Headline (Career Changer)

Example: "Teacher Turned Project Manager | Master of Organization & Communication | PMP Certified"

8. The "Mix" Headline (Student)

Example: "NYU Marketing Student | Social Media Enthusiast | Google Analytics Certified | Seeking Internship"

9. The "Result-Oriented" Headline

Example: "Increased Organic Traffic by 80% | SEO Specialist | Data-Driven Marketer"

10. The "Passion" Headline

Example: "Passionate About Making Work Simple | Process Improvement Specialist | Operations Leader"

The Secret Formula (For Your Headline)

[What You Do] + [Who You Help] + [Result You Deliver] + [Personal Touch]

Example: "I help B2B SaaS companies grow through data-driven marketing strategies | Increased revenue by 30% | Coffee lover"

What to Avoid in Your LinkedIn Headline

  • ❌ Just your job title (e.g., "Manager")
  • ❌ Unprofessional phrases (e.g., "CEO of my life")
  • ❌ Keywords only (reads like spam)
  • ❌ No keywords at all (invisible in search)
  • ❌ Emojis (unless you're in a creative field)
  • ❌ Being too vague (e.g., "Passionate professional")

Quick Tips to Make Your Headline Stand Out

  1. Include your strongest keywords – Use the ones recruiters search for.
  2. Quantify your impact – Numbers make you stand out.
  3. Keep it under 220 characters – LinkedIn truncates longer headlines.
  4. Make it scannable – Use the "|" or "•" symbol to separate keywords.
  5. Avoid buzzwords – "Guru," "Ninja," "Rockstar" are overused.

Final Thoughts

Your LinkedIn headline is a critical piece of real estate. Use these formulas and examples to craft a headline that gets you noticed and remembered.

FAQ

1. What is the best LinkedIn headline format?

The best format is: [Job Title] | [Key Skill 1] | [Key Skill 2] | [Experience Level]. It's searchable and professional.

2. How long should my LinkedIn headline be?

Under 220 characters. Longer headlines get truncated, so keep it concise.

3. Should I include emojis in my headline?

Only if you're in a creative field. For most professionals, emojis look unprofessional.

4. Can I use my current job title only?

You can, but it won't be searchable. Add keywords to appear in recruiters' searches.

5. How often should I update my headline?

Whenever you change jobs, gain new skills, or shift your career focus.
